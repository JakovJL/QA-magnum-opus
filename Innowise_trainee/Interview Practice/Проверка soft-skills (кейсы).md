# Проверка soft\-skills \(кейсы\)

## 🔹 Client Communication & Expectation Management

**Case — Last-minute client changes**

- **Situation:** The client sent major changes to a task one day before the deadline, affecting half of the work.
- **Question:** What would you do?
- **Skills:** Expectation management, stress resilience, communication.
- **Strong answer:** Explains the impact, offers realistic options (deadline extension, feature simplification, resource reallocation), keeps a professional tone.
- **Weak answer:** Agrees blindly, blames the client, becomes defensive.

**Case — Demo mismatch**

- **Situation:** During a demo, the client said the result doesn’t meet expectations, even though requirements haven’t changed.
- **Question:** How would you handle this?
- **Skills:** Active listening, requirement clarification, flexibility.
- **Strong answer:** Clarifies the exact mismatch, documents agreements, proposes a correction plan.
- **Weak answer:** Argues or ignores feedback.

**Case — Unrealistic deadline request**

- **Situation:** The client insists: *“I need this done by Friday”*, but you know it’s not feasible.
- **Question:** How would you respond?
- **Skills:** Expectation management, negotiation.
- **Strong answer:** Explains limitations, proposes alternative scope/timeline, seeks client agreement.
- **Weak answer:** Promises without checking feasibility, avoids clarifying.

**Case — Scope creep**

- **Situation:** The client keeps adding small “just one more” tasks during sprint execution.
- **Question:** How do you handle it?
- **Skills:** Boundaries, requirement management, Client Priorities understanding, team capacity vs velocity.
- **Strong answer:** Acknowledges request, negotiates backlog reprioritization, keeps sprint focus intact.
- **Weak answer:** Accepts everything without pushing back, lets deadlines slip.

**Case — Negative feedback from client**

- **Situation:** The client says: *“Your team doesn’t understand our business needs”*.
- **Question:** What would you do?
- **Skills:** Active listening, empathy, clarification.
- **Strong answer:** Asks for concrete examples, shows willingness to improve, schedules a clarification session.
- **Weak answer:** Gets defensive, shifts blame.

## 🔹 Team Collaboration & Conflict Resolution

**Case — Technical disagreement**

- **Situation:** Two developers are arguing about an architectural decision, and work is stalled.
- **Question:** What would you do?
- **Skills:** Mediation, facilitation, teamwork.
- **Strong answer:** Listens to both sides, refocuses on project goals, proposes compromise or escalates constructively.
- **Weak answer:** Ignores conflict, takes sides prematurely.

**Case — Interruptions during meetings**

- **Situation:** A teammate keeps interrupting you during meetings.
- **Question:** How would you address this?
- **Skills:** Assertiveness, communication.
- **Strong answer:** Talks privately, explains how it impacts work, suggests an agreed format.
- **Weak answer:** Stays silent or snaps back in frustration.

**Case — Blame during retrospective**

- **Situation:** During a retrospective, a teammate blamed you for delaying the release.
- **Question:** How would you react?
- **Skills:** Emotional control, constructive feedback.
- **Strong answer:** Remains calm, clarifies facts, steers conversation to process improvement.
- **Weak answer:** Gets defensive, counters with blame.

**Case — Passive teammate**

- **Situation:** One teammate is disengaged in meetings and rarely shares updates.
- **Question:** How would you deal with this?
- **Skills:** Team motivation, inclusion.
- **Strong answer:** Asks if they face blockers, encourages participation, involves them in smaller discussions.
- **Weak answer:** Ignores the issue, labels them “lazy.”

**Case — Miscommunication in remote team**

- **Situation:** Two teammates misunderstand each other in chat and the discussion escalates.
- **Question:** How would you intervene?
- **Skills:** Mediation, clarity, empathy.
- **Strong answer:** Moves discussion to a call, clarifies intent, ensures mutual understanding.
- **Weak answer:** Lets them “fight it out,” or takes sides.

**Case — Over-dominant teammate**

- **Situation:** One teammate always pushes their ideas, ignoring others.
- **Question:** How would you handle this?
- **Skills:** Facilitation, communication balance.
- **Strong answer:** Redirects conversation to ensure equal input, frames decision-making criteria objectively.
- **Weak answer:** Lets them dominate, avoids addressing imbalance.

## 🔹 Self-Management & Workload Prioritization

**Case — Out of expertise task**

- **Situation:** Your manager asks you to take on a task outside your area of expertise.
- **Question:** How would you approach it?
- **Skills:** Self-learning, adaptability.
- **Strong answer:** Breaks task into steps, researches, asks for guidance when needed.
- **Weak answer:** Refuses or takes it blindly.

**Case — Multiple urgent tasks**

- **Situation:** You have three urgent and important tasks at the same time.
- **Question:** How would you prioritize?
- **Skills:** Time management, stress resilience, Working with Priorities.
- **Strong answer:** Assesses business value, aligns with manager/client, plans execution.
- **Weak answer:** Works chaotically, tries to do everything at once.

**Case — Cross-cultural communication gap**

- **Situation:** A colleague from another country gives you short and vague answers.
- **Question:** How would you improve communication?
- **Skills:** Cross-cultural communication, empathy.
- **Strong answer:** Clarifies preferred style, asks structured questions, proposes regular syncs.
- **Weak answer:** Keeps writing the same way, waits passively.

**Case — Procrastination on a boring task**

- **Situation:** You receive a task you find boring, and you feel tempted to delay it.
- **Question:** What do you do?
- **Skills:** Self-discipline, motivation.
- **Strong answer:** Breaks it into smaller parts, finds motivators, ensures delivery.
- **Weak answer:** Delays until the deadline forces rushed delivery.

**Case — Constant context switching**

- **Situation:** Your manager keeps assigning ad-hoc urgent tasks that interrupt your planned work.
- **Question:** How do you handle it?
- **Skills:** Time management, boundary setting.
- **Strong answer:** Aligns priorities with manager, documents impact, negotiates task rescheduling.
- **Weak answer:** Tries to do everything immediately, gets overwhelmed.

**Case — Burnout signals**

- **Situation:** You notice you’re constantly tired, productivity drops, motivation fades.
- **Question:** What steps do you take?
- **Skills:** Self-awareness, resilience, wellbeing management.
- **Strong answer:** Acknowledges issue, manages workload, discusses with lead/HR, applies recovery strategies.
- **Weak answer:** Ignores symptoms, keeps pushing until exhaustion.

## 🔹 Leadership & Initiative

**Case — Team lead on sick leave**

- **Situation:** The project is stalling, and the team lead is on sick leave for a week.
- **Question:** What would you do?
- **Skills:** Leadership, initiative.
- **Strong answer:** Coordinates tasks, redistributes responsibilities, informs client of status.
- **Weak answer:** Waits for lead to return, only does personal tasks.

**Case — New team lead with different style**

- **Situation:** A new team lead with a very different management style joins the project.
- **Question:** How would you adapt?
- **Skills:** Change management, teamwork.
- **Strong answer:** Learns their working style, adjusts own communication, provides constructive feedback.
- **Weak answer:** Resists all changes, withdraws.

**Case — Missing ownership**

- **Situation:** A critical bug is blocking release, but nobody takes responsibility.
- **Question:** What would you do?
- **Skills:** Ownership, initiative.
- **Strong answer:** Steps up, gathers necessary people, assigns tasks, drives resolution.
- **Weak answer:** Waits for someone else, complains.

**Case — Process inefficiency**

- **Situation:** The team spends too much time on ineffective status meetings.
- **Question:** How do you react?
- **Skills:** Process improvement, leadership without authority.
- **Strong answer:** Suggests alternative formats, pilots shorter meetings, collects feedback.
- **Weak answer:** Keeps attending, complains privately.

**Case — Mentoring opportunity**

- **Situation:** A junior teammate is struggling with tasks you know well.
- **Question:** How would you support them?
- **Skills:** Coaching, knowledge sharing, empathy.
- **Strong answer:** Offers guidance, pairs for knowledge transfer, encourages independence.
- **Weak answer:** Ignores them, or just solves tasks instead of teaching.
